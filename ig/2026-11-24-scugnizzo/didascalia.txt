Say it in Neapolitan · Scugnizzo 🌀

Scugnizzo (shkoo-NYEET-tsoh): a Naples street kid, quick, cheeky and always grinning. Today it’s said with affection to any child with a spark of mischief. For a girl: scugnizza.

Where it comes from: in the vicoli boys played strummolo, a wooden top thrown with a string to knock the rival’s top out of the ring. The winner could “scugnare” it, split it in two, and the word stuck to the players.

History: from 27 to 30 September 1943 Naples rose against the German occupation, and boys fought beside the adults. Four of them received the Gold Medal for Military Valour after their deaths. Gennaro Capuozzo was just twelve.

How to use it: “Si’ proprio nu scugnizzo!”, you’re a real little rascal. In 1931 Raffaele Viviani brought one to the stage in L’ultimo scugnizzo.

Eat like one: fried pizza at La Masardona @lamasardona, Via Giulio Cesare Capaccio 27, near Piazza Garibaldi, frying since 1945. The classic: ricotta and cicoli, pork crackling.

Lo scugnizzo è il ragazzino di strada napoletano, sveglio e sfacciato: oggi lo si dice con tenerezza. La parola viene dallo strummolo: chi vinceva poteva “scugnare”, spaccare, la trottola dell’avversario. Nelle Quattro Giornate del 1943 quattro ragazzi, tra cui il dodicenne Gennaro Capuozzo, ebbero la medaglia d’oro alla memoria.
